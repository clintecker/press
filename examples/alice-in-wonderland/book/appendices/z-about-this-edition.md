# About this edition

This is a gallery example built by the press, not a scholarly edition. It
carries the first three chapters of Lewis Carroll's *Alice's Adventures in
Wonderland*, first published by Macmillan in 1865 and long in the public
domain. The text follows the Project Gutenberg transcription of the book,
reset into the press per-chapter format: curly quotation marks straightened,
dashes normalised to the house form, and the chapter titles cast in sentence
case, all so a real classic passes the same editorial law every book here
faces.

The plates are John Tenniel's original wood engravings, cut for the 1865
first edition. Tenniel died in 1914, and the engravings have been in the
public domain for well over a century. They were sourced from Wikimedia
Commons and set into this edition in a single black ink, the same idiom the
press uses for its own commissioned plates. Nothing in this book was
generated: the words are Carroll's and the pictures are Tenniel's.

The point of the example is narrow and practical. It proves that the
engraving path -- a plate set into the text, listed in the plates, linked
from the list, and carried into every output format -- works on a real
illustrated book with real public-domain art, and that importing such a book
from a plain-text source is the ordinary work the press is built to do.
